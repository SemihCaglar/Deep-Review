## Paper Title
SERSEM: Selective Entropy-Weighted Scoring for Membership

## Overall Decision
Accept

## Summary of Contribution
The paper introduces SERSEM, a novel white-box Membership Inference Attack (MIA) framework tailored for Large Language Models (LLMs) trained on code. SERSEM leverages selective entropy-weighted scoring to suppress uninformative syntactic boilerplate and amplify memorization signals, addressing the challenge of detecting data contamination in LLMs trained on massive, often non-permissively licensed datasets.

## Critical Review
### Methodology & Validity
The methodology is innovative, combining a dual-signal approach that utilizes both continuous character-level entropy and selective weighting to focus on informative code regions. However, the paper lacks detailed experimental results and comparative baselines, making it difficult to fully assess the robustness and generalizability of SERSEM. The absence of a thorough ablation study or statistical significance analysis further limits the evaluation of the proposed method's validity.

### Industrial Relevance
The work addresses a highly relevant problem in the deployment of code LLMs, especially regarding legal and ethical risks associated with memorization of proprietary or non-permissively licensed code. SERSEM’s approach could be valuable for organizations seeking to audit or mitigate data contamination in their models. However, practical deployment considerations, such as computational overhead and integration into existing pipelines, are not discussed.

### Clarity & Presentation
The paper is generally well-written, with a clear motivation and structured presentation. The abstract and introduction effectively communicate the problem and the proposed solution. However, the lack of detailed experimental sections and visualizations (e.g., figures or tables) reduces the clarity of the empirical validation.

## Prioritized Improvements
