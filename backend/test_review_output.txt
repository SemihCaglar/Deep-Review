## Paper Title
SERSEM: Selective Entropy-Weighted Scoring for Membership Inference in Code Language Models

## Overall Decision
Accept

## Summary of Contribution
The paper introduces SERSEM, a novel white-box membership inference attack framework tailored for code language models. By leveraging selective entropy-weighted scoring and internal transformer activations, SERSEM robustly distinguishes memorized code from boilerplate, outperforming standard probability-based baselines on StarCoder2 models.

## Critical Review

### Methodology & Validity
The methodology is well-motivated and addresses a key limitation of existing MIAs on code models: the high false positive rate due to boilerplate syntax. SERSEM’s dual approach—combining static code analysis for anomaly weighting with probing of internal transformer activations—demonstrates technical depth and innovation. The use of a balanced 25,000-sample dataset and comparison against strong baselines (Loss, Min-K%Prob, PAC) strengthens the validity of the results. However, details on dataset construction, statistical significance, and ablation studies are limited in the provided pages.

### Industrial Relevance
The work is highly relevant for practitioners concerned with data leakage and intellectual property risks in code LLMs. SERSEM’s focus on human-centric anomalies and its demonstrated improvement over existing methods make it a practical tool for auditing and evaluating code models deployed in industry.

### Clarity & Presentation
The paper is generally well-written and clearly structured. The motivation, technical approach, and results are presented logically. However, the dense formatting and occasional run-on sentences may hinder readability. The methodology section is detailed, but some implementation specifics (e.g., dataset splits, hyperparameters) are missing or underexplained in the excerpt.

## Prioritized Improvements

### High Priority
- Provide more details on dataset construction, including sources, balancing, and preprocessing.
- Include statistical significance testing or confidence intervals for reported improvements.
- Clarify the ablation of individual components (e.g., weighting scheme vs. internal activation probing).

### Medium Priority
- Improve the clarity of technical explanations, especially in the methodology section.
- Add more discussion on limitations and potential failure cases of SERSEM.
- Expand on the generalizability of the approach to other code models or domains.

### Low Priority
- Refine formatting to improve readability (e.g., paragraph breaks, clearer tables).
- Provide more visualizations or diagrams to illustrate the dual-signal methodology.
- Proofread for minor typographical errors.

## Scope Analysis

### Underemphasized Areas
- Limitations and potential adversarial adaptations to SERSEM.
- Broader applicability to non-code LLMs or multilingual codebases.
- Ethical considerations and responsible disclosure practices.

### Overemphasized Areas
- The technical description of the weighting scheme could be condensed in favor of more empirical analysis.
- Repeated justification of boilerplate suppression, which is well-motivated early on.
